# Design resources study: 10 galleries, applied to Sayable

Captured 2026-11-08, headless Chromium 1440x900. Frames are in `docs/design-resources-study/<site>/` (4.2 MB). All ten loaded without login. One land-book detail page hit a Cloudflare bot check and was dropped. Cookie banners cover parts of the reallygoodemails and adfolio frames.

Sayable has no customers yet, so every proof idea below uses evidence Sayable can produce itself: its method, sample artifacts, public standards. No invented logos, quotes or metrics.

---

## 1. land-book.com
**Accessible:** yes. The gallery loaded; one website detail page hit a Cloudflare challenge.
- The grid is sorted by page type (Landing, Pricing, Case Study, About...), so visitors browse by the job a page does (`land-book/01-top.jpg`, `d1-case-study-a.jpg`).
- Case-study pages lead with a short named headline over a large artifact image, with dense body copy underneath ("Brenntag: Supporting an empowered brand relaunch", `d1-case-study-a.jpg`).
- Strong B2B heroes pair a plain promise with a real product surface, e.g. "The work finds itself and gets done" next to a task UI (`03-scroll2.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **/readiness-audit:** structure it like a case study without a client: headline the deliverable, a large image of the inventory spreadsheet, then "what you get".
- **/:** keep the hero's product surface real (the scan log or inventory), not abstract art.

## 2. supahero.io
**Accessible:** yes. It is now part of screensdesign.
- The detail page shows one hero at full size with a single headline, one subline, one input and CTA, and a secondary text link ("or book a demo") (`supahero/d1-better-stack-a.jpg`).
- Dark, high-contrast heroes with one accent colour dominate the library (`02-scroll1.jpg`).
- The examples that work best have a headline of six words or fewer.

**For Sayable**
- **/:** one primary CTA (Request a Readiness Audit) and one quiet text link (See a sample inventory); drop any third action.
- **/:** a tight, outcome-first headline, with deadline and scope in the subline.

## 3. pricingpages.design
**Accessible:** yes.
- Detail pages tag each example with patterns such as "Custom Pricing", "Enterprise Pricing", "Contact Sales option" and "FAQs" (`pricingpages/d1-stytch-a.jpg`).
- Stytch's Enterprise column has no number: it shows "Custom", then a checklist of concrete inclusions (SLA, migration support, HIPAA/BAA), then "Contact Sales" (`d1-stytch-b.jpg`).
- Stytch puts "Need help estimating usage? Contact our dev success team" right beside the calculator, so there is help at the point of doubt (`d1-stytch-b.jpg`). Ramp leads with "Start for free. Start the savings." above three plain tiers (`d2-ramp-a.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **/readiness-audit:** a "Scope and pricing" block like Stytch's custom column: "Scoped to your document count", the five deliverables as checkmarks, "What changes the estimate" (page count, scanned vs. born-digital, departments), one CTA. It answers the price question without a number.
- **/readiness-audit:** a procurement FAQ beside the CTA: how cities buy this (purchase order, sole-source thresholds), what Sayable needs from IT, the timeline.

## 4. socialproofexamples.com
**Accessible:** yes. It is one long page of carousels grouped by proof type.
- "Wall of impact" is a grid of metric tiles with micro case studies, e.g. Ramp's "$250K saved" (`socialproofexamples/01-top.jpg`).
- Testimonial video reels (`03-scroll2.jpg`) and first-reaction videos (`04-scroll3.jpg`) need real customers.
- Third-party badges (G2) borrow credibility from an outside authority (`05-scroll4.jpg`).

**For Sayable** (no customer proof available)
- **/verify:** the badge pattern with standards Sayable can cite truthfully (WCAG 2.1 AA, PDF/UA, the DOJ Title II rule), as a "checked against" strip linking to each source, never as certifications.
- **/:** a wall of method instead of a wall of impact: tiles with checkable process facts (checks per PDF, deliverables, turnaround as a commitment). No numbers until measured.
- **/resources:** a downloadable synthetic sample inventory, clearly labelled "sample", stands in for testimonials.

## 5. reallygoodemails.com
**Accessible:** yes. Cookie banner over the lower half of the frames.
- Confirmation and transactional emails share one structure: brand mark, a big status headline ("Your order is in", "It's on the Move"), one button, then an itemised summary (`reallygoodemails/d1-confirmation-b.jpg`).
- In the library, plain-text-forward SaaS emails sit next to heavy promotional ones. The legible ones lead with one sentence and one CTA (`03-scroll2.jpg`).
- Templates are grouped by lifecycle step: Confirmation, Welcome, Activation, Thank-you (`d1-confirmation-a.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **Confirmation email:** status headline "We have your request", an itemised "What happens next" with dates, one button (add the intro call to your calendar), a named reply-to person; accessible HTML plus a plain-text part.
- **Confirmation email:** echo what the city submitted (department, approximate document count, deadline) as the summary block.

## 6. deck.gallery
**Accessible:** yes. Detail pages need joining, so I only browsed the index.
- The best report covers use one huge typographic title with a year or edition marker ("Youth Trends Report 2024", "Project Earth Report 2022") (`deck-gallery/03-scroll2.jpg`).
- Categories (Report, Guideline, Pitch) are labelled on every card (`01-top.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **/resources:** give the sample Readiness Audit a dated report cover ("Title II PDF Readiness, 2026 edition") so procurement has something to forward.
- **/resources:** tag each resource by type (Guide, Sample deliverable, Checklist).

## 7. posts.design
**Accessible:** yes.
- Announcement posts are a single statement card or one product screenshot with the brand mark. Many are data diagrams, like the timeline in `posts-design/01-top.jpg`.
- Its own hero states a concrete, counted claim ("See what 1,080+ brands actually ship"). The count is real because it is the gallery's own inventory (`01-top.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **OG images / social:** a reusable statement card (one sentence on the deadline or a finding type, plus the mark) for LinkedIn posts to clerks and ADA coordinators.
- **/solutions/government:** a timeline diagram (rule published, compliance dates by population, today) instead of a paragraph. Real dates are countable, honest proof.

## 8. adfolio.design
**Accessible:** yes. Cookie and newsletter overlays sit over the frames.
- B2B ads that work name a pain in plain words with one literal visual: "Employees overspending on travel?" over an OVER BUDGET sign, "Account Takeovers Suck" (`adfolio/03-scroll2.jpg`).
- Its filters are Industry, Platform, Offer and Angle, which separates *what you offer* from *how you frame it* (`01-top.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **/solutions/government:** a literal pain line ("A resident can't read your council agenda.") with a real PDF symptom, such as a scanned page with no text layer.
- **OG images:** write each OG headline as an angle (pain, deadline, deliverable), not the page title.

## 9. navbar.gallery
**Accessible:** yes.
- It is sorted by nav type: Static, Dropdown, Mega Menu, Announcement, Breadcrumbs (`navbar-gallery/01-top.jpg`).
- Strong B2B navs keep 4–5 items and put a single filled CTA at the far right ("Book a call", "Contact sales"). Mega menus only appear when a company has dozens of products, as with Cloudflare (`03-scroll2.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **Header nav:** static, no mega menu: about five items (Product, Readiness Audit, Government, Resources, Verify) and one filled CTA, "Request an audit". One offer doesn't need dropdowns.
- **Header nav:** an optional thin announcement bar with the next Title II compliance date.

## 10. ogimage.gallery
**Accessible:** yes. Outbound links only, no detail pages.
- The best B2B OG images are one headline plus one real product crop (Amperos: "Pursue claims, resolve denials..." with a UI panel) or a wordmark on a quiet texture (ElevenLabs) (`ogimage-gallery/03-scroll2.jpg`).
- Text sits large and on the left, so it stays legible at thumbnail size. Busy illustration ones fall apart when small (`01-top.jpg`).

**For Sayable**
- **OG images:** replace the single root `opengraph-image.tsx` with per-route images (/readiness-audit, /solutions/government, /verify, /resources): a left-aligned headline of 8 words or fewer plus a real inventory or scan-log crop. Check legibility at 600x315.

---

## Ranked: 8 highest-impact changes

1. **"Scope and pricing" block on /readiness-audit** (Stytch's "Custom" column): deliverables, what changes the estimate, one CTA.
2. **Sample deliverable as proof** (/resources, /readiness-audit): a synthetic, clearly labelled sample inventory and plan with a dated report cover.
3. **Procurement FAQ next to the audit CTA:** how cities buy, what IT provides, the timeline.
4. **Rebuilt confirmation email:** status headline, the submitted details echoed back, dated next steps, one action, a named reply-to.
5. **Per-route OG images:** a short left-aligned angle headline plus a real product crop.
6. **"Checked against" standards strip on /verify:** WCAG 2.1 AA, PDF/UA, DOJ Title II, linked to sources and never called certifications.
7. **One hero CTA plus one text link on /, and a static five-item header** with one filled CTA.
8. **A literal pain line and a compliance timeline on /solutions/government.**
